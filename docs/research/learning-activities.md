# Learning activities: evidence and implementation decisions

Research extension checked on **2026-10-27**. This supplements [learning-science.md](learning-science.md), rather than replacing its mechanism-level evidence register. The user-supplied Consensus review was used for discovery and checked selectively against primary sources; see [the audit](consensus-review-audit.md). No application, educational experiment, or effectiveness evaluation was implemented.

## What matters for Memoquill

An activity format is not a learning mechanism. A flashcard can be passive answer exposure or effortful recall; a multiple-choice question can require inference; a written answer can be copied without understanding. Select the activity for the intended performance, provide enough instruction to make an attempt meaningful, make feedback useful, and revisit/application-check the objective later. Do not rank heterogeneous study effect sizes as a league table of app features.

The existing four-family model remains appropriate: **recall**, **choice**, **constructed response**, and **procedure**. Rich variants and instructional phases make these useful across subjects without adding a database table or special engine for each study technique.

## Recommended activity portfolio

“Essential” below means a supported authoring/session capability, not a compulsory step in every lesson. Teaching choices remain configurable. Expected benefits are hypotheses for this implementation, not measured Memoquill gains.

| Format / variant | Intended learning and evidence | Foundation decision / assessment limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recall-before-reveal cards | Retrieve a defined target, then compare with a reference; use spacing and feedback. Existing S1/S3/S4/S6/S22 support the mechanisms. | **Essential**, recall family. Persist attempt/reveal/help separately. Card completion or a self-rating does not prove understanding/application. |
| Cloze / fill-in | Retrieve precise forms, relationships or missing steps in context. General retrieval evidence does not establish universal superiority of cloze. | **Essential bounded variant**, already in scope. Show original context; avoid grading a memorized cue as independent production. No automatic conversion of every document into clozes. |
| Short answer / free recall | Generate an answer without supplied alternatives. A1 shows benefits and dependence on retrieval success, not a universal win over choice. | **Essential**, recall or constructed family according to objective. Use reference/rubric and honest self-assessment when deterministic checking is unsuitable. |
| Single-choice application/discrimination | Select/explain an answer with cues; can involve recall, inference and reasoning. Format success depends on question/distractor quality and feedback (A1/A2). | **Essential**, choice family. Useful for misconception discrimination and lower-entry practice; never label it automatically inferior or equivalent to uncued production. |
| Explain why / explain a relationship | Generate causal/conceptual connections, then inspect a reference or rubric (A3). | **Essential constructed-response preset**, optional within a session. Focused prompts, not a compulsory essay after every fact. Preserve partial/unknown outcomes. |
| Prepare and teach back | Study with an explicit intent to explain to a novice, then deliver a written explanation. Preparation/expectancy matters in A4. | **Low-cost optional V1 preset** within constructed response. No social platform, AI listener or audio recorder required; no universal “learn by teaching” guarantee. |
| Worked example → completion → independent problem | Inspect a sound solution, fill a meaningful missing step, then solve a new problem with less support. Existing S10/S11 and A5 support configurable guidance. | **Essential phase/template support**, not another activity family. Keep explanation/example a resource, assistance visible, and final independent performance distinct. |
| Diagnose and repair an error | Find a flawed reasoning/code/procedure step, explain and correct it, then compare with a verified correction (A6). | **Essential authorable preset**, constructed family. Clearly label deliberately erroneous examples and include a correct resolution; do not seed uncorrected misinformation into ordinary review. |
| Bounded attempt before instruction | Attempt a carefully selected problem, compare approaches, receive explicit instruction and retry. Productive failure evidence A5 is conditional and materially more positive than a blanket “instruction must always come first”. | **Optional V1 sequence**, using existing phases. Requires a subsequent explanation/consolidation; never equate productive failure with leaving novices stuck. |
| Varied application / contrast sets | Apply an idea with changed values/context, choose between confusable principles and explain the choice. Existing S9/S15/S16 distinguish transfer from repeated-item accuracy. | **Essential authoring/selection capability** in existing choice/constructed families. Mix related objectives deliberately; do not shuffle unrelated subjects as a universal improvement. |
| Low-stakes quiz / topic assessment | Sample selected objectives, expose gaps, then act on feedback. A quiz is a session configuration, not a separate mechanism. | **Essential**, already in scope. Freeze item versions/help policy and distinguish diagnostic practice from independent assessment. No universal mastery percentage. |
| Procedure rehearsal and actual practice | Reconstruct/consult steps, perform the task where appropriate, record observations and revisit after non-use (A8; existing S19/S20). | **Essential**, procedure family. Label rehearsal, self-reported execution and independently observed performance separately. A checklist does not certify safe cooking or professional competence. |
| Matching, sorting, ordering / Parsons-style puzzles | May scaffold discrimination and sequence construction; successful rearrangement is a different outcome from unaided creation. | **Conditional** generic variant after a real authoring need and accessible keyboard implementation. No Python-only engine or assumption that drag-and-drop itself improves learning. |
| Concept maps, drawings and diagrams | Potentially useful representations/generative responses; benefits and authoring demands depend on the task. No dedicated drawing-effect review was completed in this extension. | Use resources/attachments/text alternatives first. A native canvas/graph editor remains **post-V1**; this is a scope/complexity decision, not evidence that drawing is ineffective. |
| Reading, highlighting and summaries | Reading/source access establishes background; passive completion is not retrieval evidence. Summaries can be useful resources or assessed constructed responses when appropriate. | Preserve accessible original reading and notes. Do not make every learning action a quiz, or advertise highlighting/time-spent as verified retention. |

## Evidence extension register

Qualitative labels are task-directed appraisal, not a completed formal GRADE/RoB assessment. A meta-analysis does not automatically remove bias, indirectness or dependence. Studies sharing a dataset, a preprint and its journal version, or overlapping reviews do not constitute independent replications.

| ID / finding | Design, verified result and access | Applicability → decision → future check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A1 — Format superiority is conditional** | Smith & Karpicke: four experiments, total N=372, one-week verbatim/inference tests. All retrieval formats helped versus study-only; little/no short-answer/hybrid advantage in three experiments, with advantage when initial short-answer success improved in the fourth. Author-hosted full paper read. | Educational text tasks, not every subject. Offer both formats and help/retrieval-success information; check delayed uncued and application outcomes separately. |
| **A2 — Feedback needs useful information** | Van der Kleij et al.: computer-based feedback meta-analysis, 40 studies/70 effects; elaborated feedback had larger reported effects than correctness-only feedback. Publisher abstract/metadata checked; full methods were not accessed. | Different controls/outcomes prevent an exact UI treatment prescription. Show explanation/reference/rubric and correction opportunity; evaluate whether users can fix the error and later apply the idea. |
| **A3 — Self-explanation is a defensible option** | Bisra et al.: 64 reports/69 effects, pooled g=.55. Publisher abstract checked; this is not a trial of Memoquill's prompt. | Prompt/task/prior-knowledge differences matter. Add focused explanation presets; measure understanding/transfer and burden, not explanation length alone. |
| **A4 — Teaching preparation matters** | Kobayashi: 39-study meta-analysis; teaching-expectancy moderator g=.48 versus −.02 without expectancy; overlapping additional analysis is not independent replication. Publisher abstract checked. | Do not infer that telling a learner to “teach back” at any moment guarantees benefit. Offer an explicit preparation goal and later explanation; evaluate independent understanding. |
| **A5 — Problem-first can work when designed properly** | Sinha & Kapur: 53 studies/166 comparisons; PS-I vs I-PS g=.36, 95% CI [.20,.51], with design/age/domain moderation. Abstract and selected search/design/discussion sections read. | Citation-led corpus and mixed designs bound generalization. Permit structured attempt→instruction→retry as well as example-first; require consolidation and compare meaningful outcomes, not frustration. |
| **A6 — Learning from errors needs a correction path** | Dieterich et al. 2025: systematic review of 40 studies; effectiveness depends on error highlighting/explanation, prompts, prior knowledge and complexity; long-term findings mixed. Publisher abstract/metadata inspected; full text retrieved but not comprehensively appraised. No new pooled estimate computed. | Use clearly marked flawed examples plus a sound explanation/correction. Test diagnosis and subsequent independent solution, including whether the original error was reinforced. |
| **A7 — Hint logs are not causal evidence** | An et al. 2026: multi-semester observational analysis, N=999 K–12 mathematics learners; premature/superficial hint use associated with lower gains after covariate adjustment. Author paper abstract/method framing checked. | Selection/confounding remains; do not conclude hints cause harm or remove them. Offer useful staged hints, record assistance, and evaluate strategies without compulsory delays or penalties. |
| **A8 — Procedural practice needs its own evidence** | Tatel & Ackerman 2025: meta-analysis, 457 reports/1,344 effects; non-use and decay vary by task/measure and intermittent performance. PubMed abstract checked during the independent search; full paper not reviewed. | Population averages are not an individual's cooking half-life. Support reminders, refreshed instruction and actual-performance notes; never feed procedural quiz scores into FSRS memory stability. |
| **A9 — Assisted success can hide weaker unaided performance** | Bastani et al. 2025: field experiment with nearly 1,000 high-school mathematics students; practice improved with GPT assistance, while the base-chat condition did worse when assistance was removed; guarded-tutor condition mitigated the harm. Official PNAS abstract/main text checked. | One setting/tool design is not all AI. Separate assisted practice from later unaided evidence; no inference that authoring agents must have weak application permissions. |
| **A10 — Designed AI tutoring can help specific lessons** | Kestin et al. 2025: N=194 in one college-physics course, two-lesson crossover comparison against active-class learning, favorable immediate tests for a bespoke tutor. Publisher abstract and selected design/results checked; pooled lesson observations are not independent extra students. | Short-term outcome and package-of-design differences do not establish years-long retention or an advantage for an arbitrary chatbot. Keep optional tutoring testable and source-linked; avoid product gain percentages. |
| **A11 — SRL prompting is not automatically a knowledge gain** | Fütterer et al. 2026: N=371, grades 7–9, three conditions, six 45-minute sessions. Some motivational differences, no statistically significant advantage in domain knowledge/effort/elaboration over standard ChatGPT. Publisher abstract/online date and selected excerpts checked; full study not comprehensively appraised. | This compares AI conditions, not AI versus no AI. Do not infer that all AI is ineffective or that a goal prompt is proven personalization. Preserve user control and evaluate knowledge separately from motivation. |
| **A12 — Tutor-facing support is a different intervention** | Tutor CoPilot arXiv v3: tutor-randomized study; 900 initially assigned, 783 active study tutors after attrition. Methods explicitly use session exit tickets, not a student-level end-of-year achievement estimate. Abstract, methods and limitations excerpts read. | Professional tutor mediation and varying student exposure limit direct extrapolation. Source is the arXiv version; journal status was not established. Useful design hypothesis, not proof for autonomous self-study. |
| **A13 — Supervised AI pilot does not establish unsupervised safety** | LearnLM Team/Eedi arXiv v1: exploratory RCT, N=165 across five UK schools; experts could revise each AI message. Abstract checked; detailed risk-of-bias assessment not performed. | Small developer-affiliated, human-supervised trial. Do not present it as independent proof of an unrestricted tutor or a requirement for manual review of every authoring operation. |
| **A14 — Restricting access can also have costs** | Fischer et al. 2025: working paper, randomized N=334 university learners; abstract reports favorable exam performance with AI and unrestricted access outperforming reading-first restricted access. Primary working-paper PDF abstract/design framing checked. | Short study/exam, different task and intervention from A9; not a universal rebuttal or long-term result. Keep assistance/pacing configurable; avoid a blanket lockout rule based on one paper. |

## Product consequences

1. Implement meaningful **response formats and phases**, not a large menu of unrelated engines. Existing document/resources, activity schemas, hints and session services can cover the essential portfolio.
2. Add a few explicit fields/contracts: response format, target outcome, expected answer/rubric, explanation/citation, instructional phase, solution-reveal state and assistance provenance. Keep activity revisions immutable; conditional variants still need their own schema/version when added.
3. Record **what the application observed** separately from learner-reported external help. App-controlled hint/reveal state can be recorded directly; external host assistance may be declared or unknown. Do not label an unknown condition “unaided”.
4. Separate **practice/learning mode** (examples, hints, feedback, retries) from an **independent check** (declared aids, frozen items/feedback timing). Neither constitutes certified practical competence by itself.
5. Provide formative error review and optional confidence/calibration without making every response an elaborate assessment. Correctness, self-rating, confidence and time are different data.
6. AI/MCP authoring autonomy remains governed by explicit scope/transaction policies. Pedagogical help policy is a separate layer: studies of answer assistance do not justify removing authorized content-creation capabilities. Generated content stays attributable/unverified until appropriate review; schema validation does not establish truth.

## Four example sequences

These are **engineering syntheses**, not experimentally validated Memoquill curricula. They work without AI.

- **English:** read a sense/context → recall meaning → compare feedback → produce a new sentence with a rubric → revisit later with a changed cue. A vocabulary list is not a language curriculum.
- **Python:** read a worked code example → predict/explain behavior → repair a deliberately marked bug or complete a missing step → write a new non-executed solution → compare rubric/reference → later solve a changed problem.
- **Mathematics/quantum physics:** establish prerequisite/notation → inspect a derivation or attempt a bounded preparatory problem → receive explicit explanation → complete a step → solve a new problem and explain the selected principle → delayed independent check.
- **Cooking:** inspect recipe/technique and paced demonstration where available → rehearse sequence and recall relevant facts → perform off-screen → record observations/assistance and reflect → plan a later real attempt. Keep factual accuracy and observed/self-reported execution separate.

## What is not established

No universal best question format, fixed interval, mandatory hint delay, ideal theme, or guaranteed AI advantage follows from this review. No pooled effect is a Memoquill percentage gain. No study validates the entire proposed product bundle. Dedicated drawing/diagram tooling, matching/ordering variants and individualized competence models need proportionate further evidence/usage justification, not automatic expansion of V1.
